Over the last six months, people imported more than 10,000 videos into Ghostfeed. We handpicked 1,066 references from that pile. I wanted to use them to make new videos with different creators, but first I needed a better way to find the right reference.

So this weekend I ran video understanding on the library. The goal was simple: tell me what happens in each video, what structure it follows, and what reaction the person shows. Gemini handled the video analysis. The replay below shows those saved results being sorted.

### Once we know the structure, we know which workflow to use

I gave the analysis fixed choices: reaction, lip-sync, hook + demo, talking head, and B-roll. The reaction is a separate label, such as shocked, confused, or angry. That means I can look for a particular performance instead of scrolling through a category like “beauty.”

For reaction and lip-sync, I use motion-based cloning in Ghostfeed. I want to change the creator while using the source performance as the reference. For B-roll and talking head, I use prompt-based cloning to make a new version from the scene and structure.

Hook + demo is where I combine the pieces. I clone the opening, then add my product demo in the editor. I do not need to recreate an entire reference video to use its opening.


### Here is what motion-based cloning can do

I choose the reference and the creator I want to use in Ghostfeed, then use Wan 3 Smart Motion for the motion-based clone. Here are two examples we made, with the source and the new versions shown together.

The first is a crying reaction. What I wanted to keep was the expression: the eyes, the mouth, and the small changes in the face. The comparison shows that performance on different creators. This is the kind of opening I would choose when the text needs a sad or upset reaction.

### Turn the outputs into posts

These clips give me the opening. Next I take one into the Ghostfeed editor, add the hook text, and place my product footage after it. I trim the transition so the demo follows what the opening promises.

Once that edit works, I can make versions with another creator, another first line, or a different product angle. That is how I would build a batch for Instagram and TikTok, then export the finished videos together.
